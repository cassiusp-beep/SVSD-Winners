# Branch — agent entry

Updated 18 October 2026. Read **[Product Definition.md](docs/context/product-definition.md)** for the latest direction and the five positioning answers.

The user has pivoted to a live, shared semantic map for teams collaborating on new ideas. The recommended initial niche is multidisciplinary design and engineering teams in early concept development. This niche and its underserved need are working hypotheses. The product should expose connections, differing assumptions and the development of ideas across people and time.

The strategic priority is creative discovery before commitment. Section 3 of Product Definition.md defines the counter-position: surface possible tensions and complementary contributions, preserve alternative directions, and let participants choose what to explore or commit to. Keep interface effort low; invite reflection where meaning matters. Granola also offers insights, so compare priorities rather than claiming it only produces tasks. This is a differentiation hypothesis, not an established competitive barrier.

Keep capture and draft mapping easy. Clearly distinguish live AI proposals from participant-accepted meaning and decisions. Review is needed when meaning, commitments or access changes; it must not require approving every incoming node. Preserve evidence, editable interpretation, disagreement, stable idea identity, history, optional AI and portable export. Shared-session awareness and processing scope precede live capture/AI use. The old “save first, ask about AI later” sequence does not govern a live AI session.

Accumulated value comes from a persistent project map: decision rationale, preserved alternatives, unresolved questions and outcomes that help the next session. Prioritize easy resumption and relevant reuse over node count or engagement prompts. Section 5 of Product Definition.md defines the proposed value loop and repeated-session validation. Keep evidence, review states, access boundaries and portable exports intact.

Use expressed contributions and voluntarily supplied reflections for within-person/across-person comparisons. Do not infer unspoken thoughts or rank employee creativity. The attached research informs a design hypothesis, not validated product outcomes.
