# The concept so far

Source: the team's first brainstorm on Thursday, October 15, 2026, transcribed from a recording. This is a direction, not a decision. Most of it is still open.

## The idea in one line

A web platform where creative projects are shown as living node graphs. It helps people find where they could join, fork or contribute to someone else's project, including across disciplines.

## Where it came from

- **Research tools stop at cataloguing.** Tools like Zotero let you save and highlight sources, but you can't see the journey of why you saved something or where it led.
- **Getting stuck is the trigger.** When you hit friction on a project, the product helps you find collaborators. The framing is "I'm not asking permission to use your project. I'm asking for help from you."
- **Not knowing what you're doing leads to a plateau.** Guest speakers made this point. The product should help people understand the space between their work and someone else's, not just match them.
- **Cross-discipline sparks are the fun part.** A designer posts something, someone in mechatronics says "we can make it walk," and a new hybrid appears. Classmates from costume design approach projects very differently, and that difference is valuable.

## Key ideas from the brainstorm

- **Project as node graph.** Each project, and maybe each person, is a graph similar to Obsidian's graph view. It shows where the opportunities and unexplored areas are. Alex's portfolio site, where hovering a skill branches out into related projects, was a precedent the team liked.
- **The "jellyfish" interaction.** When you look at someone's work, the system "latches on" to the parts of the project you're most compatible with, then branches out to show what else you could learn or contribute. It sits between a heavy-handed advisor and a light suggestion.
- **"Domain of possibility."** The team's phrase for what the product reveals: the possible directions a project could go, not one right answer.
- **Assistance, not prediction.** The team asked, "Are we in the business of prediction or assistance?" and landed on assistance. The AI reveals potential and may help with research, but the user decides. It must not be prescriptive.
- **Modes.** A free-for-all mode for open exploration. A quick-match mode, Tinder or Hinge style, for "charrettes," which are short design sessions with someone new. A concern was raised that swiping makes you judge only what is in front of you and miss a project's potential.
- **Any kind of profile.** People can build a profile from any media: GitHub for code, Hackster for hardware, Figma for design, photos, social links. Non-technical people without a big digital presence must not be disadvantaged. Integrations should be read selectively. The product should show only what is relevant, not merge everything into one feed.
- **Browse without an account.** People should be able to look around before signing up.
- **Off-time use.** People scroll on their phones in spare moments, so this could be a design-rooted social feed.

## How it connects to storytelling

This is the weakest link so far and needs the most work before Monday. Ideas raised:

- The node graph itself is the story. It records how a project changes from first concept through each contribution over time.
- The AI's suggestions show possible futures, the narratives a project could follow next.
- Alex was going to map out "modes of storytelling" as a next step.

## Open questions

- **Who exactly is it for?** The brief requires a specific group. Candidates from the conversation include design graduate students, interdisciplinary makers, and people looking for a fun side project. Pick one.
- **What is the single storytelling sentence?** A judge should get it in one line.
- **Is it a job board, a social feed, or a creative tool?** The team noticed it was drifting toward a "B2B job board or C2C fun board." Decide what it is not.
- **What does the AI actually do in the demo?** Name the one or two AI moments worth building for real.
- **Which mode is the hero?** Graph exploration, quick-match, or something else.
