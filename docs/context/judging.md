# What the judges reward

Source: our team's research report, "Strategic Research Analysis and Competition Strategy: Silicon Valley School of Design (SVSD)." It draws on public alumni portfolios, the SVSD site and articles about the founder. Treat it as informed interpretation, not official rules. Where it disagrees with `brief.md`, the brief wins.

## Who is judging

- SVSD is run by founder Naheel Jawaid, a former Google innovation designer who worked on AR glasses and AI creation tools.
- Reviewers and mentors are senior design leaders from companies such as Apple, Meta, Google, Notion, LinkedIn and Shopify.
- The program favors self-starters with "senior-level product instinct" who can take a product from zero to one.
- The founder talks about "belief capital": the conviction to back a bold, opinionated product direction. Hedged, safe concepts read as weak.

## What wins

- **Zero-to-one framing.** A new category or a real shift in how people work. Incremental redesigns of mature apps and B2B SaaS optimizations score poorly.
- **Working, interactive prototypes.** Judges reject static wireframe grids. They want real state changes, motion timing and micro-interactions, built in code or tools like Framer, ProtoPie or Rive.
- **Clear design principles.** Winning case studies open with sharp problem framing and two or three explicit principles, then show the working solution.
- **No bootcamp templates.** Photos of affinity diagrams, raw survey data and persona posters read as generic.
- **Reel-first communication.** Short, high-impact video of the product working beats long process write-ups.

## The past winner to study

**Figma Mobile Live Comments** won the competition in October 2025. It was built in a 6-day sprint by Rachel Chen, Emmi Wu, Christina Raganit and Angelina Cao.

- **Problem:** design ideas that happen away from the desk end up scattered across Notes, Slack and screenshots.
- **Principles:** ideas must be anchored to context, captured in full without flattening complexity, and built to build on.
- **Key decisions:** the comment layer is fully separate from the canvas, so nothing is accidentally edited. "Snap to Frame" double-tap navigation. "Figma Feed" turns scattered frames into a vertical review stack.
- **Lesson for us:** three crisp principles, each decision traced back to a principle, and every interaction actually prototyped.

## Where our research and the brief disagree

- **Process.** The research says to cut methodology and lead with reels. The brief says the most important thing to show is how we explored the problem and the challenges we solved. **Resolution:** show process as decisions and turning points, not as artifacts. "We tried X, it failed because Y, so we did Z" is the format.
- **Effort split.** The research suggests 30% problem framing and 70% polish. The brief weights product thinking and depth of thinking as two of three criteria. **Resolution:** polish heavily, but only one path, and make sure the framing is sharp enough to carry the video.
- **Medium.** The research recommended a hybrid physical-digital product to play to our architecture and physical design background. **The team has chosen a web-based product.** Our spatial background can still show up in how the interface handles space, such as the node graph.
