## Turning points to feature

Collect these as they happen, so we don't have to reconstruct them on Monday.

- Prediction versus assistance. The team chose assistance because the product should reveal potential, not decide for people.
- Swiping versus seeing potential. Quick-match risks hiding what a project could become.
- Requests versus quiet notifications. We designed an approve-or-decline flow, then cut it because it contradicted "help, not permission." Branches now grow beside the original, and the creator is told quietly.
- Modals versus the Living Canvas. We initially fell back on conventional drawer sheets and modal popovers for viewing gaps and branching. In our Phase A audit against the whiteboard walkthrough, we saw this shattered the spatial feeling of a maker's studio. We executed a full shell refactor (Option B): eliminated all modals, centered Elena in an organic studio hub, mounted live dialogue snippets directly onto bezier edges, and anchored navigation to four quiet Hot Corners.
- The Glow Trap versus TouchDesigner Modularity. We initially defaulted to dark mode voids and concentric glowing circles. It read as AI bloatware rather than a crafted product. We pivoted to a warm architectural drafting bed (`#FAF9F5`), fine millimeter dot grid, and TouchDesigner-inspired modular operator cards with a distinct shape hierarchy, clean docking ports, and small gap dots that smoothly enlarge into parameter cards on hover.
